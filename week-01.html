Day 1

Learning to Think Like a Designer

Understanding Design Thinking

Week 1 began with an insightful session by Lakshmi Vera Ma'am on the Design Thinking approach.

Before this session, I had heard the term Design Thinking, but I hadn't really understood how the entire process worked. The session introduced us to the five stages of Design Thinking:

1. EMPATHIZE
2. DEFINE
3. IDEATE
4. PROTOTYPE
5. TEST

What I found interesting was that the process doesn't begin with immediately finding a solution. It begins with understanding people.

We first need to empathize with the user, understand their actual problems, define the right problem, generate ideas, turn those ideas into prototypes, and finally test them with users.

This made me realise that a good solution isn't necessarily about having the most technically advanced idea. It is about solving the right problem for the right person.

Discovering IDEO

The session also introduced me to IDEO, a world-renowned design and innovation firm known for its human-centered approach to design.

I later watched their famous video on designing a shopping cart, and that was one of the key moments that stayed with me. What fascinated me wasn't just the final shopping cart design. It was the thought process behind it—how the team observed people, understood their needs, generated multiple possibilities, experimented with ideas, and gradually moved towards a solution.

It made me realise that innovation isn't always about one brilliant idea appearing suddenly. Sometimes, it is about observing, questioning, experimenting, failing, and trying again.

Choosing Our Industry — Pharmacy

After understanding Design Thinking, we were asked to select an industry for our primary and secondary research. We discussed different possibilities, including consumer electronics, toys, and pharmaceuticals.

Eventually, our team chose the pharmaceutical industry. One reason was that we had a psychology student in our team, which gave us an opportunity to look at the industry from a behavioural perspective as well. Another reason was that pharmaceuticals are something almost everyone interacts with as a consumer at some point.

Personally, I was also influenced by the session with the Managing Director of Tulasi Pharmacy during Week 0. His journey and insights had already made me curious about the industry, so choosing pharmacy felt like a natural direction.

Mapping the Industry

We started our secondary research by understanding the pharmaceutical market and how the industry has evolved over time. As part of this, we created a poster showing the evolution of the pharmaceutical industry and presented our findings.

It was interesting to move from simply knowing that an industry exists to actually understanding how it has developed, how it operates, and what forces have shaped it.

Day 1 Takeaway: "Design doesn't begin with a solution. It begins with understanding the person who needs one."

Day 2

Looking Beneath the Industry

Understanding the Pharmaceutical Value Chain

We continued our exploration of the pharmaceutical industry on Day 2. This time, we went beyond the basic market overview and started looking at the turnover, structure, and value chain of the industry.

Understanding the value chain helped me see how many different stages are involved before a medicine finally reaches a consumer. It isn't simply:

Medicine → Pharmacy → Consumer

There are multiple stakeholders and processes involved, from research and development, manufacturing, regulation, distribution, and wholesale to retail and the final consumer. Seeing this larger ecosystem helped us understand that a problem in one part of the chain can affect several others.

SWOT Analysis

We conducted a comprehensive SWOT analysis of the pharmaceutical industry, examining its Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats. Rather than looking at pharmaceuticals only as a healthcare sector, we started looking at it as an industry with business, technological, behavioural, and consumer dimensions.

Finding the Problem

The most important part was beginning to identify potential problem statements within the industry. Instead of immediately jumping to a solution, we started asking:

  • Where are the actual gaps?
  • Who is experiencing them?
  • Why do these problems exist?

This was where the Design Thinking approach from Day 1 started becoming practical. We weren't just learning a framework anymore. We were beginning to use it.

Day 2 Takeaway: "Understanding an industry means looking beyond its products and seeing the entire ecosystem behind them."

Day 3

A Working Holiday

Wednesday was officially a holiday, but our work didn't completely stop. It became more of a working holiday, where we continued thinking about the research, discussions, and direction of our project.

It was a quieter day compared to the others, but it gave us time to process what we had learned and continue working towards the next stage.

Sometimes, progress isn't about a major activity. Sometimes, it is simply about giving an idea enough time to develop.

Day 3 Takeaway: "Not every productive day needs to look busy."

Day 4

Building My Digital Identity

Thursday took us in a completely different direction. We started working on our portfolio and personal website.

As an EEE student, I already had some exposure to programming, but building a website made me realise that knowing how to write code is only one part of the process. I started thinking about:

  • How does the system actually work?
  • How do different components connect?
  • What needs to happen for the website to function properly?

It was interesting to move from simply writing code to understanding the larger system behind it. I initially created a simple website, but I wasn't satisfied with the result. And that dissatisfaction was actually useful.

Instead of settling for something that merely worked, I started thinking about how I wanted my personal portfolio and website to represent me. So I began curating the solution rather than simply developing a webpage.

Build → Evaluate → Identify what is missing → Improve
Day 4 Takeaway: "Building something that works is one thing. Building something that represents you is another."

Day 5

Mindset, Priorities & Looking Back

Friday began with an inspiring session by Ram Kumar Sir, where we reflected on the mindset required to achieve success. The session made us think beyond technical skills and focus on how we approach our goals, our time, and our priorities.

The 80/20 Rule (Pareto Principle)

A relatively small number of inputs can often create a disproportionately large share of results. It made me evaluate where I put my effort.

SMART Goal Framework

Setting goals in a structured, actionable way: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound.

Finishing the Portfolio

After submitting the portfolio, I also started working on the blog content that I wanted to include in my personal website. The portfolio wasn't just an assignment anymore. It was becoming a way to document the journey and communicate who I am, what I have explored, and what I am learning along the way.

We eventually wrapped up the week by presenting what we had worked on throughout Week 1.
