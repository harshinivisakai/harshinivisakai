Day 1

Problem Identification & Japanese 5S Methodology

Observing Problems with Fresh Eyes

We started the week with Problem Identification, where we learned to observe problems deeply in our physical environment and think about possible structured solutions rather than rushing to conclusions.

Alongside problem identification, we were introduced to the renowned Japanese 5S Methodology — a systematic framework designed to organize spaces so work can be performed efficiently, effectively, and safely:

1. SEIRI
Sort
2. SEITON
Set in Order
3. SEISO
Shine
4. SEIKETSU
Standardize
5. SHITSUKE
Sustain

Hands-On Application: The Red Cubical

To put theory into practice immediately, we applied the 5S method to organize and manage the tools in the Red Cubical.

By sorting through necessary items, designating dedicated locations for each tool, cleaning the workspace, standardizing visual placements, and building habits to sustain order, we saw firsthand how proper organization drastically reduces wasted search time, minimizes friction, and boosts overall productivity.

Day 1 Takeaway: "Proper organization is not just about cleanliness; it is the operational foundation for efficiency, safety, and clear thinking."

Day 2

Algorithms & Think Like a Coder

The Podi Dosa Algorithm

On Day 2, we bridged daily life and computational logic by designing an algorithm for making a simple South Indian delicacy: Podi Dosa.

While it seemed like an ordinary cooking activity at first, writing down the exact algorithm forced us to deconstruct a familiar process into deterministic, step-by-step instructions. We had to consider inputs (batter, ghee, podi), preconditions (hot tawa), precise sequences of actions, and termination conditions (crisp golden brown).

Heat Tawa → Pour Batter → Circular Spread → Drizzle Ghee → Sprinkle Podi → Flip & Toast → Serve Crisp

“Think Like a Coder” Masterclass

We also immersed ourselves in the “Think Like a Coder” animated series, successfully watching and analyzing all 11 episodes.

The narrative challenges introduced foundational concepts in a memorable, story-driven format:

Logic & Conditions

Evaluating branching decisions (if/else) and deterministic rules.

Loops & Iteration

Automating repetitive patterns efficiently without redundant work.

Decomposition

Breaking complex monumental problems down into smaller bite-sized components.

The series transformed our perspective: coding is not merely syntax, but the fundamental discipline of computational thinking and structural problem-solving.

Day 2 Takeaway: "Every complex human task is an algorithm in disguise. When you break it down into clear steps, anything can be solved."

Day 3

Rapid Creative Coding: Scratch 2-Minute Meme

Block-Based Programming Under Pressure

Day 3 was an energetic and creative session. We used MIT Scratch with a specific, high-intensity constraint: create an interactive meme within just 2 minutes.

Working under tight time pressure forced us to discard overthinking and focus entirely on core mechanics: selecting sprites, scripting costumes, triggering audio soundbites, and choreographing block events.

Speed & Intuition

Rapid decision-making replaced hesitation. We learned how visual block logic lets developers test ideas instantly.

Creativity + Code

Humor and storytelling combined with event handling, timing delays, and conditional triggers.

This activity helped us explore basic programming fundamentals while proving that building software can be playful, engaging, and remarkably fast.

Day 3 Takeaway: "Strict constraints spark explosive creativity. Quick prototyping beats endless contemplation."

Day 4

App Development with MIT App Inventor: College Connect

Building “College Connect”

On Day 4, we transitioned into full mobile app creation using MIT App Inventor, developing a functional Android application named College Connect.

The primary purpose of the app was to address a real campus communication challenge: helping students easily discover, navigate, and stay updated on college activities, inter-departmental events, seminars, and important notices across campus.

Key Functional Highlights of College Connect

  • Centralized Event Feed: Aggregates announcements from multiple departments in one clean interface.
  • Intuitive UI Navigation: Clean layouts built with layout arrangements, buttons, and responsive view containers.
  • Real-World Problem Solving: Direct application of technology to streamline campus information sharing.

This activity provided invaluable practical exposure to user interface design, event-driven programming, and deployment testing on real smartphones. It showed us how technology can directly resolve everyday communication friction faced by students.

Day 4 Takeaway: "The best applications are born out of empathy for everyday student challenges."
