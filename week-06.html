Segment 01

Laser Cutting & Vector Engraving

1. Laboratory Safety & Strict Protocols

Operating high-energy CO₂ lasers requires strict adherence to thermal, electrical, and optical safety standards. Before initializing any job:

  • Exhaust Ventilation: The blower system was activated to continuously extract acrylic fumes and smoke particles.
  • Water Chiller Cooling: The CO₂ tube cooling circulation was verified to prevent thermal stress on the laser tube.
  • Active Air Assist: High-pressure lower blowing was kept engaged to suppress flames and blow debris away from the focal point.
  • Material Verification: Only laboratory-approved 2 mm transparent acrylic was used. Chlorine-based plastics (PVC) are strictly prohibited due to toxic gas emissions.

4. Result & Problem Solving

During preliminary conversion, duplicate vector contours caused localized over-burning. By pruning overlapping paths before exporting to DXF, we eliminated redundant laser passes and achieved clean, frosted white engraving on transparent acrylic with crisp character clarity.

Laser Cutting Takeaway: "Digital fabrication quality is determined during vector preparation. Clean geometry, closed paths, and calibrated power settings produce crisp physical results."
Segment 02

3D Printing & Additive Manufacturing

1. Additive vs. Subtractive Manufacturing

While subtractive CNC machining removes material from a solid billet (facing severe reach limitations with internal undercuts and organic geometries), Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) constructs complex geometries layer by layer directly from an STL polygon surface mesh.

2. Selected STL Model: Organic Toothless Dragon

We selected an intricate Toothless dragon model (bounding envelope 45.7 × 98.7 × 16.2 mm) featuring sweeping wings, horns, curled tail segments, and subtle overhangs — a rigorous test for layer stacking, overhang thresholds, and support economy.

3. Quantitative Material & Execution Results

The Gyroid infill provided isotropic mechanical integrity without excess weight. Automatic supports consumed only 0.11 g out of 8.62 g total extrusion — a minimal ratio allowing effortless post-processing without surface scarring.

Fig 7. Bambu Studio slicing result — Model: 2.81 m / 8.50 g · Support: 0.04 m / 0.11 g · Total: 2.84 m / 8.62 g · Prepare: 5m 25s · Print: 32m 21s · Total: 37m 47s.
3D Printing Takeaway: "Additive manufacturing removes conventional geometric constraints. Smart orientation and minimal support usage make complex organic prototyping fast and sustainable."
